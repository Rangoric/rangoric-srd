# Background

Background covers what a character knows and does outside adventuring: a craft they make things with, a profession they can work at, or a subject they know well. Each subject is its own skill, written with the subject in brackets, such as Background (weaponsmithing), Background (sailor) or Background (heraldry), and each has its own ranks. A subject is a craft when its main purpose is to make a thing, a profession when its main purpose is being a thing (knowing how to work as that job), and lore when it is knowing about something. Cooking makes food, so Background (cook) covers a cook's craft; a cook who runs a kitchen for a living knows the profession too. The kinds overlap, and that's fine: a subject says how a character acts and what they can do. Background is a Flavor skill (see [[rangoric-srd/skills/Skills Overview#^flavor-skills|Flavor Skills]]).

## Subjects

Every subject the SRD uses is listed here. A new subject goes on the list with a short description; use the nearest listed subject when one fits.

### Crafts

- **alchemy**: mixing acids, fire, potions' mundane cousins and other alchemical items (needs alchemical equipment)
- **armorsmithing**: making armor and shields
- **baking**: bread, pastries, cakes and pies
- **basketweaving**: weaving baskets, mats and wicker
- **blacksmithing**: working iron and steel into tools, fittings and hardware
- **bookbinding**: binding pages into books and making covers
- **bowmaking**: making bows, arrows and bolts
- **calligraphy**: fine handwriting, illumination and decorated documents
- **candlemaking**: making candles, tapers and soap from wax and tallow
- **carpentry**: building in wood, from furniture and carts to houses and frames
- **clockmaking**: making clocks, gears and small clockwork devices
- **cobbling**: making and mending shoes and boots
- **cook**: preparing food and drink, from camp meals to feasts
- **dreamweaving**: shaping the stuff of dreams (a dream creature's art)
- **gemcutting**: cutting and polishing gems
- **glassblowing**: making glass, bottles, lenses and windows
- **gunsmithing**: making and repairing firearms
- **instrument making**: making musical instruments
- **jewelcrafting**: setting gems and making jewelry
- **leatherworking**: tanning hides and making leather goods
- **locksmithing**: making locks and keys
- **mirrormaking**: making mirrors
- **origami**: folding paper into shapes
- **painting**: painting pictures, portraits and decoration
- **pottery**: shaping and firing clay
- **puppetmaking**: making puppets and marionettes
- **ropemaking**: making rope, cord and nets
- **scrimshaw**: carving bone, ivory and shell
- **sculpting**: carving or modeling statues in stone, wood or clay
- **shipmaking**: building boats and ships
- **siegecraft**: building siege engines
- **stonemasonry**: cutting and laying stone
- **tailoring**: making clothing
- **tattooing**: inking designs into skin
- **trapmaking**: building mechanical traps
- **weaponsmithing**: making weapons
- **weaving**: weaving cloth, from thread to bolts of fabric

### Professions

- **accountant**: keeping books and accounts
- **apothecary**: preparing and selling medicines
- **architect**: designing buildings and overseeing their building
- **barkeep**: running a bar or tavern
- **barmaid**: serving in a tavern or inn
- **barrister**: pleading cases before a court
- **beekeeper**: keeping bees for honey and wax
- **brewer**: brewing beer and ale
- **clerk**: keeping records for a business or office
- **courtesan**: companionship, charm and court manners for hire
- **driver**: driving carts, wagons and carriages
- **drover**: herding livestock over long distances
- **educator**: teaching students
- **falconer**: keeping and flying hunting birds
- **farmer**: working the land and raising crops and livestock
- **fisherman**: catching fish with nets, lines and traps
- **fortune-teller**: telling fortunes for pay
- **gambler**: making a living at games of chance
- **gardener**: tending gardens and grounds
- **herbalist**: gathering and using plants of every kind
- **librarian**: keeping and finding books and records
- **medium**: speaking with spirits for clients
- **merchant**: buying and selling goods
- **midwife**: delivering babies and tending mothers
- **miner**: digging for ore and stone
- **sailor**: working a ship, its rigging and its course
- **scribe**: writing and copying documents
- **showman**: putting on shows and drawing crowds
- **siege engineer**: crewing and aiming siege engines
- **soldier**: soldiering: drill, camp life and the chain of command
- **soothsayer**: reading omens and foretelling events
- **tanner**: tanning hides into leather
- **torturer**: extracting information and confessions by pain
- **trapper**: trapping animals for fur and food

### Lore

- **astrology**: the movements of the stars and constellations, the seasons in which each star rises and sets, birth signs, and reading the sky for good and bad days. It's what the astrologers of [[rangoric-srd/rules/star magic/Star Magic|star magic]] study.
- **dragons**: dragons, their kinds, lairs and hoards, and the history of [[rangoric-srd/rules/dragon-magic/Dragon Magic|dragon magic]]. It identifies dragons.
- **fey**: fey creatures and their courts, customs, bargains and weaknesses, the fey realm, and the places where it touches the world. A DC 15 check tells you whether a place is apt to be used as a [[rangoric-srd/rules/Fey Roads and Doorways#Doorways|doorway]] into the fey realm. It identifies fey, as Nature does.
- **folk tales**: the folklore and myths of one people or region, and where its uncommon place names come from.
- **heraldry**: coats of arms, noble houses, their lines and their badges
- **poisons**: poisons, their sources, signs and antidotes
- **runes**: the twenty-four runes of [[rangoric-srd/rules/rune-magic/Rune Magic|rune magic]], what each means, and how they are learned and inscribed.

## Making Things

**Check**: You can practice your trade and make a decent living, earning about half your check result in gold pieces per week of dedicated work. You know how to use the tools of your trade, how to perform the craft’s daily tasks, how to supervise untrained helpers, and how to handle common problems. (Untrained laborers and assistants earn an average of 1 silver piece per day.)

The basic function of a craft Background, however, is to allow you to make an item of the appropriate type. The DC depends on the complexity of the item to be created. The DC, your check results, and the price of the item determine how long it takes to make a particular item. The item’s finished price also determines the cost of raw materials.

In some cases, the fabricate spell can be used to achieve the results of a Background check with no actual check involved. However, you must make an appropriate Background check when using the spell to make articles requiring a high degree of craftsmanship.

A successful Background check related to woodworking in conjunction with the casting of the ironwood spell enables you to make wooden items that have the strength of steel.

When casting the spell minor creation, you must succeed on an appropriate Background check to make a complex item.

All crafts require [[rangoric-srd/equipment/gear/Artisan's Tools|artisan’s tools]] to give the best chance of success: improvised tools make the check harder, and masterwork tools make it easier.

To determine how much time and money it takes to make an item, follow these steps.

1.  Find the item’s price. Put the price in silver pieces (1 gp = 10 sp).
2.  Find the DC from the table below.
3.  Pay one-third of the item’s price for the cost of raw materials.
4.  Make an appropriate Background check representing one week’s work. If the check succeeds, multiply your check result by the DC. If the result × the DC equals the price of the item in sp, then you have completed the item. (If the result × the DC equals double or triple the price of the item in silver pieces, then you’ve completed the task in one-half or one-third of the time. Other multiples of the DC reduce the time in the same manner.) If the result × the DC doesn’t equal the price, then it represents the progress you’ve made this week. Record the result and make a new Background check for the next week. Each week, you make more progress until your total reaches the price of the item in silver pieces.

If you fail a check by 4 or less, you make no progress this week.

If you fail by 5 or more, you ruin half the raw materials and have to pay half the original raw material cost again.

_Progress by the Day_: You can make checks by the day instead of by the week. In this case your progress (check result × DC) is in copper pieces instead of silver pieces.

_Creating Masterwork Items_: You can make a masterwork item—a weapon, suit of armor, shield, or tool that conveys a bonus on its use through its exceptional craftsmanship, not through being magical. To create a masterwork item, you create the masterwork component as if it were a separate item in addition to the standard item. The masterwork component has its own price (300 gp for a weapon or 150 gp for a suit of armor or a shield) and a making DC of 20. Once both the standard component and the masterwork component are completed, the masterwork item is finished. Note: The cost you pay for the masterwork component is one-third of the given amount, just as it is for the cost in raw materials.

_Repairing Items_: Generally, you can repair an item by making checks against the same DC that it took to make the item in the first place. The cost of repairing an item is one-fifth of the item’s price.

When you use a craft Background to make a particular sort of item, the DC for checks involving the creation of that item are typically as given on the following table.

| Item                                                              | Subject        | Making DC         |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| Acid                                                              | Alchemy (1)    | 15                |
| Alchemist’s fire, smokestick, or tindertwig                       | Alchemy (1)    | 20                |
| Sunrod, tanglefoot bag, or thunderstone                           | Alchemy (1)    | 25                |
| Armor or shield                                                   | Armorsmithing  | 10 + AC bonus     |
| Longbow or shortbow                                               | Bowmaking      | 12                |
| Composite longbow or composite shortbow                           | Bowmaking      | 15                |
| Composite longbow or composite shortbow with high strength rating | Bowmaking      | 15 + (2 × rating) |
| Crossbow                                                          | Weaponsmithing | 15                |
| Simple melee or thrown weapon                                     | Weaponsmithing | 12                |
| Martial melee or thrown weapon                                    | Weaponsmithing | 15                |
| Exotic melee or thrown weapon                                     | Weaponsmithing | 18                |
| Mechanical trap                                                   | Trapmaking     | Varies (2)        |
| Very simple item (wooden spoon)                                   | Varies         | 5                 |
| Typical item (iron pot)                                           | Varies         | 10                |

| Complex or superior item (lock)                                   | Varies         | 20                |

2 _Traps have their own rules for construction._

**Action**: Does not apply. Background checks to make things are made by the day or week (see above).

**Try Again**: Yes, but each time you miss by 5 or more, you ruin half the raw materials and have to pay half the original raw material cost again.

**Special**: Dwarves are better at Background checks related to stone or metal; see the [[rangoric-srd/races/Dwarf|dwarf]].

Antitoxin and other remedies are made with [[rangoric-srd/skills/Medicine|Medicine]] instead.

Gnomes are better at Background (alchemy) checks; see the [[rangoric-srd/races/Gnome|gnome]].

Background (alchemy) is also the skill of the [[rangoric-srd/rules/alchemy-and-herbalism/Alchemy|alchemy]] rules: their processes, analysis and identification, the [[rangoric-srd/rules/alchemy-and-herbalism/Alchemical and Herbal Items|alchemical and herbal items]], and [[rangoric-srd/rules/Gems#Disguising and Detecting a Gem’s Value|disguising and detecting a gem’s value]].

You may voluntarily add +10 to the indicated DC to craft an item. This allows you to create the item more quickly (since you’ll be multiplying this higher DC by your Background check result to determine progress). You must decide whether to increase the DC before you make each weekly or daily check.

To make an item using Background (alchemy), you must have alchemical equipment and be a spellcaster. If you are working in a city, you can buy what you need as part of the raw materials cost to make the item, but alchemical equipment is difficult or impossible to come by in some places. Purchasing and maintaining an [[rangoric-srd/equipment/gear/Alchemist's Lab|alchemist’s lab]] helps with Background (alchemy) checks because you have the perfect tools for the job, but it does not affect the cost of any items made using the skill.

### Advanced Uses

The character can craft items more quickly than normal.

| Task           | DC                |
| -------------- | ----------------- |
| Quick creation | +10 or more to DC |

_Quick Creation_: A character can voluntarily increase the DC of crafting an item by any multiple of 10. This allows the character to create an item more quickly (since he or she will be multiplying this higher DC by his or her skill check result to determine progress). The character must decide the increase to the DC before making the check.

_Quick Identification_: A character with Background (alchemy) can identify an alchemical substance or potion in the field as a full-round action, without an alchemical lab or any cost, with a DC 50 check. The character can’t retry this check (or take 20); if he or she fails, the character must identify the substance in an alchemical lab, as normal. (Mysticism identifies potions too.)

| Task                       | DC (or DC Modifier) |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Create augmented substance | +20 or more to DC   |

_Create Augmented Alchemical Item or Substance_: This requires the Augmented Alchemy feat, and allows a character to create alchemical items and substances of greater power than normal. To augment an alchemical substance, add +20 to the DC required to create the item and multiply the cost by 5. If the item or substance deals damage, double the damage dealt. If the item or substance doesn’t deal damage, double the duration of its effect. If the item or substance doesn’t deal damage and doesn’t have a specific listed duration (or has an instantaneous duration), double all dimensions of its area. If the item or substance doesn’t fit any of these categories, then it cannot be improved in this manner. A character can create an item with multiple degrees of augmentation. For every additional multiplier applied to damage, duration, or area, add an additional +20 to the DC and add an additional 5 to the cost multiplier.

| Item             | Effect of Augmenting                         |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Acid             | Direct hit 2d6, splash 2                     |
| Alchemist's fire | Direct hit 2d6, splash 2                     |
| Antitoxin        | Duration 2 hours                             |
| Smokestick       | Smoke fills a 20-foot cube                   |
| Sunrod           | Glows for 12 hours                           |
| Tanglefoot bag   | Becomes brittle and fragile after 20 minutes |
| Tindertwig       | No effect                                    |

## Working at a Profession

Herbalist, apothecary and brewer are among the professions the [[rangoric-srd/rules/alchemy-and-herbalism/Alchemy and Herbalism|alchemy and herbalism]] rules use: Background (herbalist) covers all uses of plants, and Background (apothecary) their medicinal part.

**Check**: You can practice your trade and make a decent living, earning about half your Background check result in gold pieces per week of dedicated work. You know how to use the tools of your trade, how to perform the profession’s daily tasks, how to supervise helpers, and how to handle common problems.

**Action**: Not applicable. A single check generally represents a week of work.

**Try Again**: Varies. An attempt to use a profession Background to earn an income cannot be retried. You are stuck with whatever weekly wage your check result brought you. Another check may be made after a week to determine a new income for the next period of time. An attempt to accomplish some specific task can usually be retried.

**Untrained**: Untrained laborers and assistants (that is, characters without any ranks in a profession Background) earn an average of 1 silver piece per day.

## Knowing a Subject

A lore subject has to be narrow, far narrower than the Knowledge skill it sits inside. A good test is whether one person could know nearly everything about it. Heraldry, one city, one guild or one sport are narrow enough; cities, monsters or magic are not. A broad field such as [[rangoric-srd/skills/Society|Society]] covers a lore subject and much more besides. Background is how a character with only a spare point or two can still be the one who knows about it.

**Check**: Background checks about a lore subject work like any Knowledge check; see [[rangoric-srd/skills/Knowledge|Knowledge]] for the DCs, the action, trying again and untrained checks. Most questions about a narrow subject don’t get harder as you go up in level.

A lore subject about one kind of creature (dragons, undead, the giants of one range) can be used to identify creatures of that kind, as a Knowledge skill can.

A lore subject is a Knowledge skill: it counts wherever a rule says "any Knowledge skill". Craft and profession subjects aren't.

## Unlocks

You gain these only through [[rangoric-srd/feats/Signature Skill|Signature Skill]] or a class feature such as the [[rangoric-srd/classes/Rogue#^rogue-edge|rogue’s edge]]; see [[rangoric-srd/skills/Skills Overview#^skill-unlocks|Skill Unlocks]].

### Making

**5 Ranks**: When determining your weekly progress, double the result of your Background check before multiplying the result by the item's DC.

**10 Ranks**: You do not ruin any of your raw materials unless you fail a check by 10 or more.

**15 Ranks**: When you determine your progress, the result of your check is how much work you complete each day in silver pieces.

**20 Ranks**: You can craft magic armor, magic weapons, magic rings, and wondrous items that fall under your craft subject using the normal rules for making things.

### Working

**5 Ranks**: When using Background checks to earn income, you earn gold pieces equal to the result of your check each week.

**10 Ranks**: When attempting Background checks with a profession, you can roll twice and take the better result. When answering questions about your profession, you can always take 10.

**15 Ranks**: You can attempt checks to earn income once per day instead of once per week.

**20 Ranks**: When attempting Background checks with a profession, you can choose to roll once instead of twice. If you do and the result of the roll is less than 10, replace it with 10. When answering questions about your profession, you can always take 20.

### Knowing

Lore subjects use the Knowledge skills' unlocks, in [[rangoric-srd/skills/Knowledge#Unlocks|Knowledge]].
