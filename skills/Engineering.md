# Engineering

Engineering covers how mechanical things work: traps, locks and their workings, clockwork, siege engines, and how to jam, rig or take them apart. It took in Disable Device and the engineering half of Knowledge (architecture and engineering). Picking a lock is [[rangoric-srd/skills/Sleight of Hand|Sleight of Hand]]; building a trap or siege engine is [[rangoric-srd/skills/Craft|Craft]]; buildings and other structures are Dungeoneering.

## Mechanisms

**Check**: Answering a question about mechanisms, traps, siege engines, clockwork, how a device works, or the science behind them (forces and motion, how materials hold up, how things burn, corrode or react) has a DC of 10 (for really easy questions), 15 (for basic questions), or 20 to 30 (for really tough questions), the same as a Knowledge check.

Where making a creature takes engineering, such as some golems, Engineering can also be used to know about it; the GM decides case by case. Constructs are otherwise identified with Arcana.

**Action**: Usually none, as with Knowledge.

## Disabling Devices

**Check**: The Engineering check is made secretly, so that you don’t necessarily know whether you’ve succeeded. The DC depends on how tricky the device is. Disabling (or rigging or jamming) a fairly simple device has a DC of 10; more intricate and complex devices have higher DCs.

If the check succeeds, you disable the device. If it fails by 4 or less, you have failed but can try again. If you fail by 5 or more, something goes wrong. If the device is a trap, you spring it. If you’re attempting some sort of sabotage, you think the device is disabled, but it still works normally.

You also can rig simple devices such as saddles or wagon wheels to work normally for a while and then fail or fall off some time later (usually after 1d4 rounds or minutes of use).

| Device    | Time       | Engineering DC (1) | Example                                                     |
| --------- | ---------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Simple    | 1 round    | 10                    | Jam a lock                                                  |
| Tricky    | 1d4 rounds | 15                    | Sabotage a wagon wheel                                      |
| Difficult | 2d4 rounds | 20                    | Disarm a trap, reset a trap                                 |
| Wicked    | 2d4 rounds | 25                    | Disarm a complex trap, cleverly sabotage a clockwork device |

1 *If you attempt to leave behind no trace of your tampering, add 5 to the DC.*

**Action**: The amount of time needed to make an Engineering check depends on the task, as noted above. Disabling a simple device takes 1 round and is a full-round action. An intricate or complex device requires 1d4 or 2d4 rounds.

**Try Again**: Varies. You can retry if you have missed the check by 4 or less, though you must be aware that you have failed in order to try again.

**Special**: A character with trapfinding who beats a trap’s DC by a wide margin can bypass it (along with her companions) without disarming it; see [[rangoric-srd/classes/Rogue#^rogue-trapfinding|trapfinding]].

**Restriction**: Disarming magic traps takes [[rangoric-srd/classes/Rogue#^rogue-trapfinding|trapfinding]].

The spells fire trap, glyph of warding, symbol, and teleportation circle also create traps that a character with trapfinding can disarm with a successful Engineering check. Spike growth and spike stones, however, create magic traps against which Engineering checks do not succeed. See the individual spell descriptions for details.

### Other Ways to Beat a Trap

It’s possible to ruin many traps without making an Engineering check.

**Ranged Attack Traps**: Once a trap’s location is known, the obvious way to ruin it is to smash the mechanism—assuming the mechanism can be accessed. Failing that, it’s possible to plug up the holes from which the projectiles emerge. Doing this prevents the trap from firing unless its ammunition does enough damage to break through the plugs.

**Melee Attack Traps**: These devices can be thwarted by smashing the mechanism or blocking the weapons, as noted above. Alternatively, if a character studies the trap as it triggers, he might be able to time his dodges just right to avoid damage. A character who is doing nothing but studying a trap when it first goes off gains a +4 dodge bonus against its attacks if it is triggered again within the next minute.

**Pits**: Disabling a pit trap generally ruins only the trapdoor, making it an uncovered pit. Filling in the pit or building a makeshift bridge across it is an application of manual labor, not the Engineering skill. Characters could neutralize any spikes at the bottom of a pit by attacking them—they break just as daggers do.

**Magic Traps**: Dispel magic helps here. Someone who succeeds on a caster level check against the level of the trap’s creator suppresses the trap for 1d4 rounds. This works only with a targeted dispel magic, not the area version (see the spell description).

### Advanced Uses

The character can rush his or her Engineering attempt, reducing the amount of time it takes to perform the attempt.

| Reduce to              | DC Modifier |
| ---------------------- | ----------- |
| 1 round                | +20        |
| Move-equivalent action | +50        |
| Free action            | +100       |
