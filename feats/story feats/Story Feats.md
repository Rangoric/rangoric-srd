# Story Feats

A story feat ties a character’s past to the campaign. Each one starts from an event in the character’s life, gives a benefit straight away, sets a long-term goal, and gives a further benefit when the goal is reached. Story feats carry the tag *story*.

Every story feat has these parts:

- **Prerequisites**: at least two ways to qualify; meeting any one of them is enough.
- **Benefit**: what the feat gives as soon as it is taken.
- **Goal**: what the character sets out to do.
- **Completion Benefit**: what the feat gives once the goal is reached, on top of the benefit unless it says otherwise.

### Taking a Story Feat

You can take a story feat whenever you gain a feat, but you can have only one uncompleted story feat at a time. Choose it with the GM, who may adjust it to fit the campaign. Story feats are meant for player characters, but NPCs and monsters can take them too.

The GM works the feat’s story into the campaign and brings it up every few sessions. If the campaign is unlikely to resolve a feat’s story, the GM can shift its goal to something the character can reach: a meaningful story matters more than the feat’s exact wording.

Allies can help you complete a story feat, but you must lead the tasks it calls for. If you don’t, the GM may add to the goal or rule that it isn’t completed.

### Common Rules

Story feats use these terms in their prerequisites and goals.

**Appropriate Number**: Creatures whose CRs add up to at least 20 or five times your character level, whichever is higher. This uses your current level, not your level when you took the feat. An encounter with a CR 3 or more below your level doesn’t count, unless circumstances made it much harder. ^appropriate-number

**Challenging Foe**: A foe, or a group of foes, with a total CR of at least 10 or your character level + 3, whichever is higher. A particular individual’s CR is set when you take the feat, and rises as you gain levels, usually by 1 for every one or two levels you gain. ^challenging-foe

**Character Level**: Your character level. For a creature better described by its CR (most monsters with more than 1 Hit Die), use its CR instead. ^character-level

**Decisively Defeat**: Overcome a foe by killing it, knocking it unconscious, or taking it prisoner. You must be a significant part of the fight, even if someone else lands the final blow. Driving a foe off while doing it little harm doesn’t count; whether a foe that flees counts is up to the GM. ^decisively-defeat

**Slay**: Kill a foe, or destroy, petrify, or banish it to another plane, or remove it by similar means that only powerful magic can undo. Unless the feat says otherwise, you must deal the final blow yourself. ^slay

**Thwart**: Disrupt a foe’s plans substantially and essentially for good, such as by deposing a lord. You must be a significant part of it. You keep the feat’s benefits even if the foe later returns. ^thwart

**Stacking**: Most bonuses from story feats are untyped and stack with most other bonuses. Untyped bonuses from two story feats don’t stack with each other: use only the higher. ^story-feat-stacking
