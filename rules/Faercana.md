# Faercana

The arcana that mortal wizards study are nothing compared to fey magics and faercana for one simple reason: while wizards’ arcana provide power, faercana are in many ways the lifeblood of many fey creatures. It is still a focused look at magics and how they operate to change the rules of reality, but to the faeries and their ilk, it is as essential as breathing.

## Magical Power Sources

It seems strange to some mortals that fey spellcasters would want or even need sources from which to draw magical power. After all, they and their home realm are so suffused with magic… and that is the reason the fey often use power sources. Whereas mortal sorcerers pull magic from the very air around them or from within themselves, magic to faeries and their ilk is life; to haphazardly pull magic could possibly harm the land or someone they know, so some rely more on power sources.

Magical power sources work very much like spell components, although each of the standard fey power sources has its quirks and benefits (as well as drawbacks). The three most common sources that fey use to manipulate magic are the Breath of Life, true names, and magic sand.

### Breath of Life

> [!warning] Evil
> Harvesting and using the Breath of Life is evil. It steals the breath (Constitution) of mortals and fey, often killing them, and the Unseelie who use it prefer children. It belongs to villains; a character who takes a Breath of Life commits an evil act.

The malevolent Unseelie fey are barred from using *magic sand* to help power their spells. Instead, they harvest the Breath of Life from mortals and fey alike. Most often, victims are left severely weakened if not killed outright by having their breath stolen from them, but this matters little to the Unseelie. In fact, they relish what they have stolen all the more if it kills the donor. Their favorite donors are mortal children and infants, as they insist the unused potential in their lives powers their spells even more. (Each Breath of Life equals a point of Constitution.)

More potent than its counterpart, the Breath of Life can be captured with the *[[rangoric-srd/spells/Harvest Breath of Life|harvest breath of life]]* spell.

When a caster uses a Breath of Life, he gains an effective caster level for 1d4 rounds for each Breath used. Each use of Breath is considered a separate act and the bonuses do not stack, so a caster cannot use three Breaths to temporarily gain three levels. The Breath of Life also does not stack with other temporary bonuses to caster levels.

### True Names

Every person has a name, whether it is one given after birth, earned through valorous deeds, or given as a mark of punishment. All people also have true names with which they are born. Most mortal individuals and many fey, however, do not know their true names and need to undergo great amounts of research to obtain them. Learning someone else’s true name is an even greater undertaking, since much of the research into one’s true name is personal introspection and soul-searching.

If a person obtains another’s true name, he can use it against them in spells. Conversely, if you use the wrong true name, or someone else’s name, the spell does not have any additional effects above the normal spell. The most common use of true names comes in the summoning, binding, and banishing of demons and other outsiders, as invoking their true names often calls them up from the Lower Planes.

**Defending your true name**: Characters all have a +30 sacred bonus to keep someone from learning their true names. This is an automatic bonus, and cannot be negated in any way. For every level above 5th, this bonus goes up by one. For example, a 17th-level character has a +42 sacred bonus.

**Discerning your true name**: Characters must devote 1d12 years of devoted meditation and breathing exercises to truly get to know their body and their true self before learning their true name. Barbarians, monks, rangers, and sorcerers, being more in tune with their own selves than other classes, reduce that time by 1d6 years. Additionally, anyone can learn their true name by fully dedicating time to meditation and introspection; for each month spent in solitary meditation, a character comes one year closer to his true name.

**Mind-reading a true name**: Using psionics or spellcasting, someone can read a true name only if the person himself knows it. Even then, the +30 sacred bonus is only reduced to +20 in such instances.

**Using a true name**: If used in the casting of a spell, a true name provides one of the following effects:

- Reduces any of the target’s spell resistance by 10 or by half, whichever is greater;
- Adds a +20 DC penalty to resist or counter the spell cast at them;
- All normal saves against such a spell are reduced to a maximum of +1, though any supplemental saving throw bonuses still apply (not racial or ability bonuses).

**Extracting a true name from a spell**: If a true name is used in the casting of a spell, it can be discerned by others with a [[rangoric-srd/skills/Identifying Magic|Magic skill]] check (DC 33).

**Alternate Uses**: Obviously, knowing someone’s true name can be a very valuable tool. Often overlooked are the more mundane uses for such knowledge. A person can be held at bay out of fear that somehow their true name will be revealed to others. A person could also be blackmailed into performing certain tasks in exchange for not divulging a true name to other people.

### Magic Sand

*Magic sand* is an arcane substance refined from the crystallized soil of a desert in the fey realm. In the fey realm, *magic sand* is not very rare; in fact, it can be found in markets as commonly as bread is in a mortal marketplace (but not as cheaply). In the mortal world, however, it is expensive, as the magical properties leach out of the material rather quickly.

The properties and uses of *magic sand* are quite broad, whether it is used in the fey realm or the mortal worlds. Its definite and rumored powers are noted below, and in the *[[rangoric-srd/spells/Harvest Magic Sand|harvest magic sand]]* spell. ^magic-sand

- Using *magic sand* (whether on its own or as *fey dust*) always incurs a slightly random effect. Whenever it is in use, the GM rolls on [[#^table-magic-sand-wild-characteristics|Table: Magic Sand Wild Characteristics]] and invokes the random effect it gives.
- *Magic sand* is most commonly used as a universal spell component by the fey; in fact, the [[rangoric-srd/feats/Fey Magic|Fey Magic]] feat makes it usable as a component for any spell.
- Using *magic sand* as a spell component always adds +1 per die of the spell effect, whether it is damage or healing or the number of creatures affected. If there are no dice in the spell’s effects, it adds +1 per spell level to the DC to resist the spell’s effects.
- Adding one ounce of *magic sand* per spell level in addition to the normal spell components imbues the spell with the effects of either [[rangoric-srd/feats/Empower Spell|Empower Spell]] or [[rangoric-srd/feats/Spell Penetration|Spell Penetration]].
- In mortal worlds, *magic sand* can be worth up to 100 gp per ounce (5d20, depending on region and local supply and demand). In the fey realm, *magic sand* is sold for up to 100 sp per ounce (5d20).
- *Magic sand* can be used to make magic items. *[[rangoric-srd/magic-items/dusts/Fey Dusts|Fey dusts]]* are the most common sand-derived items. Rumors and legends suggest some fey craftsmen can forge very strong and very magical glass weapons and armors out of *magic sand*, though few have ever been seen.
- Mortal wizards use *magic sand* as a universal component in making magic items. Each ounce of *magic sand* can be used instead of 150 gp worth of gems or other rare materials. It cannot be used to form the item itself unless one makes *fey dust* or other magical dusts and powders. Using the sand generally reduces the time to create an item by up to 25% (5% per ounce used).

#### Magic Sand Wild Characteristics

Whenever *magic sand* is used in its raw form or in any refined crystalline form (such as *fey dusts*, but not if infused into a solid form), the magics within it cannot be wholly controlled or contained. Add one of the following effects for each use of an ounce of *magic sand*, and roll an additional time if more than three ounces are used at once.

*Example*: Using *magic sand* as a universal component in a *[[rangoric-srd/spells/Fireball|fireball]]* spell only requires one check against wild characteristics, while casting a *[[rangoric-srd/spells/Summon Monster V|summon monster v]]* with *magic sand* forces two rolls (a 5th-level spell takes 5 ounces) to determine random effects to either the caster, target, or environs.

*Persons Affected*: For each use of the *magic sand*, roll d4 to determine who is affected by the wild characteristics detailed below: 1 = user; 2 = target; 3 = both; 4 = neither (the closest living targets outside of the normal area of effect, be they plant or animal). If durations are not mentioned under the effects, they match the duration of the spell cast with the *magic sand*.
