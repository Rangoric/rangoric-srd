# Spellsinging

Spellsinging is the art of working magic through song, enabling magicians to weave spells by creating music instead of mystic gestures and words. Spellsingers use the flow of a melody to control arcane energies and at times modify them beyond the ability of normal mages. While spellsingers rarely work large or flashy spells, they can accomplish a lot with subtle magic that is mistaken as a mere performance. The art of spellsinging is found either in civilized regions where bardic colleges are available to provide training or amongst primitive peoples where songs are still used in the mystical or religious traditions of the tribe. While the training of both types of spellsingers is radically different, the resulting skills are much the same. In either case, spellsingers are well respected by their peers, for their magical abilities and their musical prowess.

Spellsinging was once a far more common skill that it is now. In the early days of arcane magic, spellsingers were able to control their magical energies more effectively by using music as a focus. Despite the effectiveness of spellsinging, the years of musical study, added to the years of magical study, proved a formidable deterrent for many would-be spellsingers. Instead, these magicians focused on refining arcane magic itself until a focus such as spellsinging was unnecessary, leaving it as an area of study now only pursued by a small number of magicians.

## What belongs to spellsinging

- **Prestige class**: the [[rangoric-srd/prestige-classes/Spellsinger|Spellsinger]], who casts spells as [[rangoric-srd/prestige-classes/Spellsinger#^spellsinger-spellsinging|spellsongs]] (sung or played, checked with [[rangoric-srd/skills/Performance|Performance]]), widens them with [[rangoric-srd/prestige-classes/Spellsinger#^spellsinger-singing-radius|singing radius]], and writes them as spell sheet music.
- It builds on the bard’s [[rangoric-srd/classes/Bard#^bardic-music|bardic performance]] or the skald’s [[rangoric-srd/classes/Skald#^skald-raging-song|raging song]].
