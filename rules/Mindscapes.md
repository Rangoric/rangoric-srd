# Mindscapes

Mindscapes are temporary constructs of the mind that come into being on the [[rangoric-srd/rules/Planes#The Astral Plane|Astral Plane]] and fade away again, much as a sleeper’s imagined landscape forms and dissolves while he dreams. The main difference between a mindscape and a dream is intent: a creature often builds a mindscape deliberately and precisely, while a dreamer usually doesn’t. A mindscape can come into being when creatures fight a [[rangoric-srd/rules/Psychic Duels|psychic duel]], and through certain spells (such as _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Create Mindscape|create mindscape]]_), magic items, rituals and other occult phenomena.

In theory, a mindscape can take any form or appearance and have any conceivable trait, much as the planes do. In practice, mindscapes often look mostly normal, or have only one or two traits that stray from reality, and the psychic avatars of creatures within might not be able to tell they’re no longer in the real world.

There are two kinds of mindscape: binary and immersive. **Binary mindscapes** happen during [[rangoric-srd/rules/Psychic Duels|psychic duels]], when a creature with psychic powers draws another into a mental battle. Only two participants can occupy one. This arena doesn’t fully engulf the participants’ senses: a creature in a psychic duel in a binary mindscape can still perceive the real world, but is largely consumed by its psychic battle. The mindscape is a powerful image in the mind’s eye, perceived abstractly and able to affect the creature in the real world, detaching it from real-world events while leaving it able to perceive them.

**Immersive mindscapes** are far more tangible and realistic. In one, a creature’s every sense perceives its imaginary surroundings completely, in effect replacing its body’s real perceptions. While a creature’s consciousness is within a mindscape, its body in the real world can take no actions and loses its Dexterity bonus to AC, but it isn’t [[rangoric-srd/rules/Special Abilities and Conditions#^helpless|helpless]], as the unconscious parts of its mind still resist its destruction. In an immersive mindscape, the mind gets no information about what the body sees, hears, smells or touches, so if the body is hurt by an attack in the real world, the mind doesn’t know. A binary mindscape, by contrast, lets a creature keep track of its own condition in the real world.

## Binary Mindscapes

A binary mindscape is usually only a minimal backdrop for a psychic battle: a ghostly image, like a stark memory, lacking detail and realism. It might be a flat surface surrounded by fog, or a featureless plain of grass on a cloudy day. Sometimes it mimics a real place its creator knows well, but even then the details at the edges of vision are usually fuzzy, and features of the lesser senses, such as smell and taste, are missing. A binary mindscape has no effect on the psychic battle; it is only a mental construct that helps the participants picture their actions.

**Creation and Control**: A binary mindscape is created when two psychic creatures enter a mental confrontation. Only creatures able to cast _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Instigate Psychic Duel|instigate psychic duel]]_ can begin a psychic duel; once the connection is made, the binary mindscape is created. Within it, a creature can expend psychic energy to create mental hazards called [[rangoric-srd/rules/Psychic Duels#Psychic Manifestations|manifestations]], to damage the other combatant or reshape the mental landscape. Because the mindscape becomes a shared mental space once it’s created, neither participant truly controls it, and both have an equal ability to alter it once their minds are connected.

A binary mindscape is overt, finite and harmful (see [[rangoric-srd/rules/Mindscapes#Mindscape Traits|Mindscape Traits]]). Its other traits (such as gravity, time and magic) are normal, apart from the special actions and spellcasting limits of the [[rangoric-srd/rules/Psychic Duels#Running Psychic Duels|psychic duel rules]].

## Immersive Mindscapes

An immersive mindscape is a less common but far more powerful kind. When created, it seems every bit as real and vivid as the real world: a creature within can see the land, feel the breeze, hear the rain, smell the sea, and even feel hunger and thirst. Sometimes creatures in one don’t even realize where they are. A target fooled into believing such a mindscape is real wastes away in the physical world while living a full life in the mindscape.

An immersive mindscape can be sculpted in much more detail than a binary one, and its traits vary. Its creator sets its traits, and visitors are subject to whatever limits the host can place on it. It might be carefully designed, or a more instinctive expression of its creator’s desires or intentions (conscious or not). Some spells let the creator use a target’s memories or desires to build the mindscape, the better to fool the target.

## Mental Masks

Creatures drawn into mindscapes don’t have to appear as themselves, and can take alternate forms called mental masks instead. They can do so only if they know they’re in a mindscape or believe they’re dreaming (the creator of a mindscape almost always does). A mental mask lets a creature hide its true identity, masking its true features from its enemies. Non-psychic creatures can’t take a mental mask; they appear in immersive mindscapes in their true forms.

## Mindscape Traits

Each mindscape has its own traits that set its appearance and behavior, as the planes do. These override the Astral Plane’s normal [[rangoric-srd/rules/Planes#Planar Traits|planar traits]]. Often they mimic the Material Plane’s, since that is what creatures within a mindscape usually find most familiar and comfortable. A creature’s mental avatar has the same physical characteristics in the mindscape that the creature has in real life (the same AC, hit points, ability scores and so on). Weapons and armor, even magical ones, work inside the mindscape.

Every mindscape responds to thought, but instead of changing by its own will, it responds to the will of those inside it. Usually the creature that created it controls its traits. Depending on her expertise, the creator can alter one or more of its traits to suit her purposes. The [[rangoric-srd/rules/Psychic Duels|psychic duel rules]] and some spells let other creatures alter mindscapes, but usually only if they know they’re inside one.

### Transparency

A mindscape’s creator usually knows when she is in her own mindscape, but other creatures might not, depending on the mindscape.

_Overt_: An overt mindscape is obvious to anyone drawn into it, perhaps because its host deliberately invites others, or because targets know they’re under psychic attack and caught in the mindscape, and so understand what has happened. A creature that knows it’s in a mindscape can leave it more easily (with _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Mindscape Door|mindscape door]]_), but knowing doesn’t make an immersive mindscape any less real to it: it can still be affected by the mindscape, and can still take damage or gain conditions in a harmful one (see Feedback). Binary mindscapes are always overt.

_Veiled_: The veiled immersive mindscape is the most insidious kind. Its triggers are designed to fool the target, trapping it so it doesn’t realize its reality is the product of its own or another’s mind. The creator’s memories are the basis of the mindscape, and any place or phenomenon she hasn’t experienced might be hard to recreate convincingly from imagination alone.

Psychic mindscape traps and spells often include a seamless move from the real world to the mindscape to keep up the veil. For example, a chest trapped with a veiled immersive mindscape might trigger a continuation of the current scene, making a thief believe she still kneels before the chest in the room where she found it. If the effect is done well and is powerful enough, the thief might live out hours, days or weeks trapped in her own mind while her body stays slumped before the chest, slowly dying from lack of food and water.

### Shape and Size

When the mindscape is manifested, its creator decides the shape and size of the mental place.

_Finite_: A finite mindscape has clear boundaries and limits to its space. Either there’s no way to move beyond them, or there’s simply nothing beyond them. It might be a hollow within an endless expanse of stone, or the inside of a cottage with nothing beyond the doors and windows but blackness. Binary mindscapes are always finite.

_Infinite_: An infinite mindscape stretches on forever, or seems to for all practical purposes: an endless three-dimensional void, perfectly flat ground stretching as far as the eye can see, or an endless ocean. Single objects within it might have limits, such as a building in the middle of an endless plain or chunks of rock floating in the void.

_Self-Contained Shape_: A mindscape with this trait might seem to go on forever, but its space folds back on itself whichever way creatures travel. It might be a staircase that somehow loops, a winding tunnel that looks straight but starts and ends in the same place, or a tesseract where leaving by one side of the cube always returns the traveler through the opposite side. A self-contained keep might let travelers leave by the front gate only to find themselves coming back in by the rear door.

### Feedback

Though they’re mental constructs, mindscapes can sometimes cause physical effects through mental feedback. A creator with exceptional psychic power might make a mindscape harmless to her but harmful to everyone else, but most mindscapes affect everyone the same way.

_Harmful_: Injuries and conditions inflicted on creatures in a harmful mindscape are real. Psychic attacks harm the body by convincing the brain the damage is real. Binary mindscapes are always harmful.

_Harmless_: Occupants of a harmless mindscape still feel the environment or other creatures harming them, but the injuries and conditions aren’t real: they do no physical harm to the body. A creature that dies in a harmless mindscape wakes up none the worse for it.

### Gravity

The creator or controller of a mindscape decides whether it has gravity, and if so, how strong it is.

_Normal Gravity_: Gravity works just as it does on the Material Plane.

_Light Gravity_: Gravity is weaker, and creatures can jump, fly and lift heavy objects with less effort.

_Heavy Gravity_: Gravity is stronger, and jumping, flying and lifting objects take more effort.

_No Gravity_: There is no gravity. Objects float wherever they are in relation to one another unless something sets them moving, in which case they travel in a straight line at a constant speed until they hit another object or a creature changes their motion.

_Subjective Gravity_: Gravity works however each creature wishes, relative to that creature. If one creature stands on a parlor’s floor while another thinks of its ceiling as “down” and stands on it, each feels gravity differently and sees the other as overhead and upside down.

### Time

Controlling time in a mindscape is hard. Usually time flows at the same rate as in the real world; only a powerful psychic can change that.

_Normal Time_: Time flows normally.

_Slow Time_: The creator slows time, so those within spend more real-world time on what they do in the mindscape: 1 round in the mindscape takes 2 or more rounds in the real world.

_Rapid Time_: The creator speeds time, so 2 or more rounds of activity in the mindscape pass while only 1 round passes in the real world. A controller could spend a long time contemplating a complex mental puzzle, then leave the mindscape and rejoin her allies having lost no real time at all.

### Alignment

Some mindscapes have a palpable resonance that reflects their creator’s alignment, intended or not. Most mindscapes aren’t aligned.

_Mildly Aligned_: A mildly aligned mindscape has very subtle effects that its inhabitants might or might not notice, such as an orderly garden for a lawful creator, or a serene bit of countryside with ideal weather for a good one.

_Strongly Aligned_: A strongly aligned mindscape almost always has noticeable features. The atmosphere alone might discomfort or even damage creatures of an opposed alignment. A creator who wants a strongly aligned mindscape might find it hard to keep victims from noticing these features; the creeping alignment influence can warn those trapped within that all is not as it seems.

### Magic

Magic, particularly psychic magic, behaves oddly in a mindscape. Beyond the options below, the [[rangoric-srd/rules/Psychic Duels#Running Psychic Duels|psychic duel rules]] say how magic works in binary mindscapes during psychic duels.

_Normal Magic_: In an immersive mindscape with normal magic, characters and creatures can use spells, spell-like abilities and magic items as usual. Spells are used up and charges or consumables spent. Damage dealt by magic is real, and the body in the real world suffers it if the mindscape is harmful. But magic that needs physical handling (such as drinking a potion) might not behave as expected: the character could “drink” the potion and find nothing happens. Some divinations might give strange or nonsensical answers about what is happening in the real world while the caster’s mind believes it is elsewhere. When a creature leaves a mindscape, any magic it used inside has been used up.

_Dead Magic_: Magic might not work at all in a mindscape. The effort to summon and shape the energies a magic needs can be blocked, kept from reaching through the psychic barrier of the creature or thing that created the mindscape. The mindscape may or may not fake results to fool those trapped within (for example, making healing magic seem to work when it doesn’t). Whatever happens inside, creatures leave without having used up any magic. The one exception is psychic spells made specifically to manipulate a mindscape, which work even in a dead magic mindscape and are used up normally.

_Altered Magic_: Magic might behave very differently in a mindscape; use the enhanced, impeded, limited or wild [[rangoric-srd/rules/Planes#Magic Traits|magic traits]] of the planes.
