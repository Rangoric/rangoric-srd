# Possession

These rules don’t add new mechanics; they explain the finer points of possession magic, as used by the _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Possession|possession]]_ spell, monster abilities and other spells.

## What Is Possession?

True possession is when a creature displaces or overrides the target’s consciousness with its own, taking direct control of the target’s body. Several effects are often confused with possession; this section describes possession and the effects like it, and how they differ.

**Domination**: _Dominate_ spells (such as _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Dominate Person|dominate person]]_) are often confused with possession, as they have superficially similar results, but domination and possession aren’t the same. Domination is mind control: it enslaves the target’s mind and forces it to carry out the caster’s will, so the caster doesn’t directly control the target’s body. The dominated mind carries out the caster’s orders with the means, knowledge and experience it has.

**Spell Possession**: The classic possession spell is _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Magic Jar|magic jar]]_, which lets the caster detach her soul from her body and place it in another creature’s body, displacing the host’s soul and leaving her own body essentially empty. Like _possession_, these effects belong to the necromancy school, as befits magic that manipulates life force and souls. This is true possession, since the result is control of the host’s body rather than its mind. _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Marionette Possession|Marionette possession]]_ works on the same principle, but doesn’t displace the host’s soul, because the target gives up control to the caster willingly.

These forms of possession carry great risk for the caster. She has no way to survive without a body to inhabit, so if her soul is ejected from the host’s body while her own body is out of the spell’s range, she dies instantly.

_Possession_ follows rules like those of _magic jar_ and _marionette possession_, with a few key differences. It doesn’t displace the host’s soul when the possessor seizes control of a creature’s body. And the caster doesn’t die instantly if her body is out of the spell’s range when she is ejected: the range of _possession_ is only how far the caster can be from her intended target when she casts it. Once ejected, the caster’s soul snaps back to her body from any distance, as long as it is on the same plane.

**Incorporeal Possession**: [[rangoric-srd/monsters/ghost/Ghost|Ghosts]], shadow demons and similar creatures don’t have physical bodies; they are disembodied souls. They use the rules below for how their mental characteristics interact with their hosts’ physical forms. Such a creature merges with the host’s body and is unharmed when ejected. Creatures that use _magic jar_ can often use _possession_ instead.

## Mental Abilities

_Possession_ holds the core mechanics of possession. _Magic jar_ says the possessing creature keeps her “mental abilities”, and _possession_ works the same way. Here, “mental abilities” means the following.

- **Skills and feats**: The possessor uses her own skill ranks, and any of her feats she still qualifies for in the host’s body. She doesn’t gain the host’s feats or skill ranks, but does apply the bonuses and penalties that come with the host’s body. For example, when flying, a character possessing a bird would use her own ranks in [[rangoric-srd/skills/Acrobatics|Acrobatics]], but the bird’s Dexterity modifier and racial, size and maneuverability bonuses.
- **Spells and spell-like abilities**: The possessor can use spells and spell-like abilities. Spells still need their components and foci. Some spell-like abilities are racial, but the soul’s essence temporarily instills the possessor’s nature into the host’s body: a shadow demon possessing a paladin can still use its racial spell-like abilities.
- **Class abilities**: The possessor can use nonmagical and magical class abilities, such as domain, hex, rage and school powers. Supernatural abilities other than class abilities aren’t mental abilities, since they generally rely on a creature’s physical form: a red dragon possessing a cleric couldn’t use its breath weapon while inhabiting the cleric’s body. The GM can make an exception for an ability she thinks is solely mental.

## Rule Interactions

Several effects let one creature influence or control another, and it isn’t always clear how they play out against each other, whether possession against compulsion or even possession against possession. They interact as follows.

**Possession and Charms and Compulsions**: Possession overrides charms and compulsions that control a creature. Neither stops the other from working, but possession bypasses the mind and takes direct physical control of the body, which may leave a possessor enslaving a mind that a compulsion has already made essentially helpless. Charms and compulsions move with the mind or soul of the creature they first targeted. For example, if you cast _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Mind Swap|mind swap]]_ on a dominated creature, the domination stays active but moves with the dominated mind to its new body; and a dominated creature able to possess could possess another creature while staying dominated.

If the host’s mind or soul isn’t displaced from the body, a dominating creature can still interact telepathically with its now-possessed target, even though the target is helpless within its own hijacked body. The dominating creature can even command the target to explain what the host body is doing, if the host can use its senses.

Likewise, a caster can target a possessing creature with a charm or compulsion. If the possessor is the only mind or soul in the host body, the effect works on it normally, and if the possessor is later evicted from the body, the effect stays active on it when it returns to its own body. For instance, if a [[rangoric-srd/classes/Mesmerist|mesmerist]] is using _mind swap_ on a bard, and a succubus uses _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Dominate Monster|dominate monster]]_ on the possessed bard, the mesmerist must attempt the save; if he fails, he is dominated by the succubus, and when his possession ends and he returns to his body, he is still at her mercy.

When both the host and a possessor occupy a body at the same time, a charm or compulsion can target either one. But the caster must be aware of the possessor’s presence to target it; otherwise, the effect targets the host, usually to little effect.

**Possession and Divinations**: Possession does nothing to hide from or block most divination spells. For example, if an evil [[rangoric-srd/classes/Mesmerist|mesmerist]] is possessing a paladin, _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Detect Evil|detect evil]]_ senses an evil creature when the paladin’s body enters its area. The [[rangoric-srd/feats/Hidden Presence|Hidden Presence]] feat can help a possessor foil divinations. If a creature’s body and soul are in two places, as with a caster of _magic jar_, divinations that depend on location, such as _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Locate Creature|locate creature]]_ or _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Scrying|scrying]]_, find nothing. The exception is _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Discern Location|discern location]]_, which gives both locations unless the body and soul are protected by _[[rangoric-srd/spells/Mind Blank|mind blank]]_ or a deity.

**Possession and Possession**: If you try to possess a creature that is already possessed, the possessing creature must succeed at the save against your possession or be ejected, letting you enter the host. If the possessing creature chooses to fail its save, it is ejected automatically and the host then attempts the Will save instead. A possession effect that allows no save automatically ejects the possessing creature.
