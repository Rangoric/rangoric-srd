# Broken

An item with the broken condition is less effective at its designated task. An item gains it when an effect says so, such as a firearm [[rangoric-srd/equipment/Firearms#Misfires|misfire]]. (Pathfinder also breaks an item that has taken damage in excess of half its hit points; Rangoric keeps the 3.5 rule for now, where an item is destroyed at 0 hit points, as [[rangoric-srd/combat/Combat Maneuvers#Sunder|sunder]] says.) The broken condition has the following effects, depending upon the item.

- If the item is a weapon, any attacks made with the item suffer a –2 penalty on attack and damage rolls. Such weapons only score a critical hit on a natural 20 and only deal ×2 damage on a confirmed critical hit.
- If the item is a suit of armor or a shield, the bonus it grants to AC is halved, rounding down. Broken armor doubles its armor check penalty on skills.
- If the item is a tool needed for a skill, any skill check made with the item takes a –2 penalty.
- If the item is a wand or staff, it uses up twice as many charges when used.
- If the item does not fit into any of these categories, the broken condition has no effect on its use.

Items with the broken condition, regardless of type, are worth 75% of their normal value. If the item is magical, it can only be repaired with a *[[rangoric-srd/spells/Mending|mending]]* or *[[rangoric-srd/spells/Make Whole|make whole]]* spell cast by a character with a caster level equal to or higher than the item's. Items lose the broken condition if the spell restores the object to half its original hit points or higher. Non-magical items can be repaired in a similar fashion, or through the [[rangoric-srd/skills/Craft|Craft]] skill used to create it. Generally speaking, this requires a DC 20 Craft check and 1 hour of work per point of damage to be repaired. Most craftsmen charge one-tenth the item's total cost to repair such damage (more if the item is badly damaged or ruined).
