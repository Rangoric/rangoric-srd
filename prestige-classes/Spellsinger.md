## Prestige Classes

### Spellsinger

Most spellsingers are bards because of their talent both magical and musical. All spellsingers have some training as bards and spellsingers are common in many bardic colleges, adding to the already great affinity between them. Sorcerers, wizards and rogues are other classes likely to follow the path of the spellsinger. The sorcerer is probably the best able to master the arts of spellsinging, but anyone with musical training can eventually learn them.

Spellsingers spend their time seeking to learn more of the spellsinging arts that have been lost. This takes them into dark and dangerous places where ancient lore has lain untouched for millennia. Because of this, they work with adventurers in seeking to learn more of their craft. Spellsingers fund these efforts by relying on their musical skills–or adventuring ability–to earn them a living wage.

Spellsingers practice [[rangoric-srd/rules/spellsinging/Spellsinging|spellsinging]].

**Hit Die**: d4.

###### Requirements

To qualify to become a spellsinger, a character must fulfill all the following criteria.

**Skills**: [[rangoric-srd/skills/Performance|Performance]] 7 ranks, 5 ranks in a [[rangoric-srd/skills/Identifying Magic|Magic skill]].

**Feats**: Any one [[rangoric-srd/feats/Feats Overview#Metamagic Feats|metamagic feat]].

**Spellcasting**: Ability to cast 3rd-level arcane spells.

**Special**: [[rangoric-srd/classes/Bard#^bardic-music|Bardic performance]] or [[rangoric-srd/classes/Skald#^skald-raging-song|raging song]] class feature.

###### Class Skills

The spellsinger’s class skills (and the key ability for each skill) are [[rangoric-srd/skills/Arcana|Arcana]] (Int), [[rangoric-srd/skills/Dungeoneering|Dungeoneering]] (Int), [[rangoric-srd/skills/Engineering|Engineering]] (Int), [[rangoric-srd/skills/Nature|Nature]] (Int), [[rangoric-srd/skills/Perception|Perception]] (Wis), [[rangoric-srd/skills/Performance|Performance]] (Cha), [[rangoric-srd/skills/Society|Society]] (Int), [[rangoric-srd/skills/Theology|Theology]] (Int).

**Skill Points at Each Level**: 4 + Int modifier.

###### Class Features

All of the following are class features of the spellsinger prestige class.

**Weapon and Armor Proficiency**: A spellsinger gains no proficiency with any weapon, armor or shield. The spellsinger is an arcane spellcaster but doesn’t suffer the usual chance of [[rangoric-srd/equipment/Equipment Armor|arcane spell failure]]; see spellsinging, below.

**Spells per Day**: Spellsingers continue their training in arcane magic as per their original class, except now they can work their magic through song. When a new spellsinger level is gained, the character gains new spells per day as if she had also gained a level in an arcane spellcasting class she belonged to before becoming a spellsinger. She does not gain any other benefit a character of that class would have gained, such as class abilities, saving throws, or attack bonus. ^spellsinger-spells-per-day

**Spellsinging (Su)**: Spellsinging is the art of weaving arcane spells using song instead of mystic gestures and ancient phrases. Once a character takes a level of spellsinger she may specify any of her prepared spells as spellsongs instead of normal spells. This does not change the level of the spell slot required, as metamagic feats do. If the character does not prepare spells, as with bards and sorcerers, she may choose at the time of casting to make any spell a spellsong. ^spellsinger-spellsinging

When casting a spellsong, the process varies slightly depending on whether the character’s Performance skill is vocal (sing) or instrumental. Characters who sing treat all their spellsongs as having no somatic components and instead they all have verbal components. Thus characters who sing do not suffer the chance of arcane spell failure for armor, but wearing any type of helmet gives a 10% chance of spell failure due to sound distortions caused by the helmet.

Characters that play instruments treat their spellsongs as having a somatic and verbal component. They suffer the normal chance for spell failure but receive a +1 bonus to their effective caster level. The character must have the appropriate instrument on hand to make use of this ability.

To cast a spellsong, the character must meet the spell component requirements of the spell, which makes *[[rangoric-srd/spells/Silence|silence]]* incredibly effective against spellsingers. The spellsinger must make a [[rangoric-srd/skills/Performance|Performance]] check against DC 10 + the level of the spell being cast. If this fails, the spellsong is expended for the day, but it does not go off, much like a failed [[rangoric-srd/rules/Concentration (Check)|concentration check]]. If the Performance check succeeds, the spellsinger then makes whatever concentration checks are necessary and suffers all appropriate attacks of opportunity. If the concentration checks are successful, the spell is cast.

A spellsinger casting spells appears to most people to simply be singing or playing a musical instrument, but anyone may make a [[rangoric-srd/skills/Identifying Magic|Magic skill]] check (DC 20 + the level of the spell being cast) to notice arcane undertones in the song.

Any spellsong with a duration of concentration requires the caster to keep making music of some type while concentrating. If the spellsinger is affected by *silence* while concentrating, any spells being maintained end immediately.

**Bardic Performance**: A spellsinger’s class levels add to her bard levels for her rounds per day of [[rangoric-srd/classes/Bard#^bardic-music|bardic performance]] (“bardic music” in older rules; see [[rangoric-srd/classes/Bard#^bardic-music-in-older-rules|bardic music in older rules]]), or to her skald levels for her rounds per day of [[rangoric-srd/classes/Skald#^skald-raging-song|raging song]]. ^spellsinger-bardic-performance

**Greater Countersong**: At 2nd level, a spellsinger is able to counter the effects of sound-based magic more effectively than normal bards. Any Performance checks made in order to use the [[rangoric-srd/classes/Bard#^countersong|countersong]] ability receive a +5 competence bonus. ^spellsinger-greater-countersong

**Pierce Silence (Su)**: At 3rd level, a spellsinger receives a +2 natural bonus on any saving throw made to resist magical silence. ^spellsinger-pierce-silence

**Singing Radius**: At 4th level, when preparing spells (or when casting them, for sorcerers and bards), the spellsinger may choose to increase the range of her spells to affect everyone nearby. This acts as a metamagic feat that increases the spell slot required for the spell, and the spell must be prepared or cast as a spellsong. Using this ability in all ways functions exactly like using a metamagic feat. Everyone within the radius is affected by the spell, be they friend or foe. ^spellsinger-singing-radius

*Singing radius I* (4th level) allows the spellsinger to cast spells with a range of personal with a radius of 10 feet, at a cost of two spell levels higher than the original spell.

*Singing radius II* (7th level) allows the spellsinger to cast spells with a range of personal or touch with a radius of 20 feet, at a cost of three levels higher than the original spell.

*Singing radius III* (10th level) allows the spellsinger to cast spells with a range of personal, touch or close with a radius of 30 feet, at a cost of four levels higher than the original spell.

*Example*: A spellsinger wishes to prepare *[[rangoric-srd/spells/Mage Armor|mage armor]]*, a 1st-level spell, using singing radius III. This requires a 4th-level spell slot, and when cast it affects everyone in a 30-foot radius of the spellsinger.

**Spell Shout (Sp)**: At 5th level, once per day the spellsinger may use the spell shout ability to break through a magical silence for one round. The spellsinger may make noise for one round and then returns to being silenced. ^spellsinger-spell-shout

**Accompaniment**: At 6th level, when accompanied by other musicians the magical power of the spellsinger grows, allowing her to perform much greater magical feats when working with a large group of magicians. To use this ability the characters accompanying the music of the spellsinger must spend a standard action in the same round to accompany the spellsinger. They must also succeed at a Performance check (DC 15). If the accompanist’s Performance check is successful, the spellsinger’s effective caster level is boosted by +1 for that spell for each person accompanying the spellsinger. Accompanying a spellsinger provokes an attack of opportunity. ^spellsinger-accompaniment

**Pierce Countersong (Su)**: At 8th level, when countersong is used against the spellsinger, the person using countersong takes a –10 circumstance penalty on the roll when using it against the spellsinger’s spellsongs. ^spellsinger-pierce-countersong

**Orchestrate Spellsong**: At 9th level, the spellsinger can create spell sheet music, or *arcane orchestras* as they are more commonly called. These function as scrolls but require a Performance check (DC 15 + the level of the spell) to cast from the sheet music successfully. Anyone with the Performance skill may use spell sheet music, even if they are not able to cast arcane spells or do not have the [[rangoric-srd/skills/Emulation|Emulation]] skill. Creating a piece of spell sheet music follows the same rules as creating a scroll ([[rangoric-srd/feats/Scribe Scroll|Scribe Scroll]]). ^spellsinger-orchestrate-spellsong
